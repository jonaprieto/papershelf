What it does

Four things, and it tries to do them without asking for your library in return.

One home for every paper

Browse the folders you already use, or add a single PDF. Sources stay separate, searchable and easy to revisit — and stay exactly where they are.

Names you can trust

Every rename is proposed first, with what it was built from and where the year came from. Originals move to original_pdfs/ and the Trash is always recoverable.

Highlights that mean something

A small palette you name yourself — definition, evidence, doubt, follow-up — so a colour carries a reason. Notes attach to the passage, and the list beside the page follows you as you read.

AI when it helps

Suggested names and answers come from the endpoint and key you set. Before anything is sent, the window says how many documents, roughly how many words, and to which host.

Reading projects

Ask across the papers you chose

Group a chapter's worth of reading into a project and ask it a question. Every claim in the answer carries a numbered source, and every source shows the passage it came from — click it and the page opens at it.

You tick which documents go. The line above the box counts both — 12 of 14 documents — because that is the whole point of choosing.

Where your papers stay

On your machine

The library is a file in your Application Support folder. There is no account, no sync and no server belonging to us.

Your endpoint, your key

Text reaches a model only when you ask a question or ask for a name, at the endpoint you configured, billed by whoever runs it.

Nothing borrowed

PaperShelf bundles no third-party code. It is built on the frameworks that ship with macOS, and it is MIT licensed.
